# M7 research record — automatic facial preparation

Research date: 2026-10-08. This document records a decision gate, not a prepared avatar or M8 implementation. The original M6 GLB stays outside the repository. No paid request or user credential was used.

## Evidence from the accepted M6 asset

The local file `original-reconstruction.glb` in the user's Downloads folder was inspected read-only with `node scripts/m7-inspect-glb.mjs <file>`. Its SHA-256 is `9e6cacfd20f6938b665084370759a09a9b05b6b7d63340bcef8e836407514767`. The user confirmed this is the accepted M6 result. This fingerprint identifies the inspected asset without committing the private geometry or texture. Its measurements are **one sample**, not promises about the topology, size, UVs, materials or proportions of future reconstructions.

The raw accessor bounds are X `[-0.362, 0.355]`, Y `[-0.254, 0.284]`, Z `[-0.856, 0]`. Orientation is not semantically labeled. The single material and watertight welded surface mean that a local face replacement must identify the correct facial region, open the eye/mouth space, and join the new region cleanly. Geometry counts cannot certify whether the face, hair, ears or rear silhouette look good.

For 53 float32 position morphs, storing one delta per indexed M6 vertex would require about **183 MB** before base geometry, normals, textures and GPU overhead (`287705 × 53 × 3 × 4`). The published ICT FaceKit Light _full face area_ has 9,409 vertices, so the equivalent upper-bound position payload for that patch is about **6.0 MB**. Actual export should include only controls used by `AvatarControlState` and may use compression or quantization after visual checks. These figures are storage arithmetic, not a Safari runtime benchmark.

## Real template topology and seam spike

The official [ICT FaceKit Light repository](https://github.com/USC-ICT/ICT-FaceKit) was cloned into a temporary directory for read-only inspection; none of its 444 MB checkout or mesh assets are committed. `node scripts/m7-inspect-facekit.mjs <FaceXModel-dir>` verified that the neutral OBJ and sampled `eyeBlink_L/R`, `jawOpen`, `mouthSmile_L`, `browInnerUp_L`, `mouthFunnel` and `mouthPucker` OBJ files each have **26,719 vertices and identical 26,384-face index topology**. This is stronger evidence for morph-template construction than a documentation claim alone.

Both published facial regions have four boundary loops: one outer boundary, one mouth opening and two eye openings. The narrower 6,706-vertex / 6,560-face region has an outer loop of 138 vertices; the 9,409-vertex / 9,230-face full region has an outer loop of 204 vertices. The internal mouth and eye loops have 74, 42 and 42 vertices in both regions. Sampled maximum motion of the _outer_ boundary in ICT template units:

| Shape           | Narrow outer boundary | Full-face outer boundary |
| --------------- | --------------------: | -----------------------: |
| `eyeBlink_L`    |                 0.008 |                    0.006 |
| `jawOpen`       |             **1.402** |                **0.181** |
| `mouthSmile_L`  |                 0.248 |                    0.133 |
| `browInnerUp_L` |                 0.213 |                    0.193 |
| `mouthFunnel`   |                 0.233 |                    0.029 |
| `mouthPucker`   |                 0.226 |                    0.035 |

The wider face region is the **initial M8 patch choice** because it substantially reduces outer-boundary motion for jaw and lip actions. The numbers are in the stock template's units and are not a prediction of fitted-avatar seam motion. Even its boundary moves, so a stationary cut edge would tear: M8 must carry residual motion into a short transition band on the Hunyuan shell or constrain/taper compatible deltas while retaining expression quality. Hair, ears and rear skull remain unchanged outside that band. The four loops also show that the template has actual eye and mouth openings; it is not a flat face plane. No fit or seam on the Hunyuan asset has yet been demonstrated.

## M8 implementation direction and rejection gates

1. For **each new** M6 avatar, keep its exact original Blob/metadata immutable. Produce its own **derived** asset and versioned preparation manifest. All geometry work happens on user device in a worker/WASM-capable pipeline; no application backend, WebGPU requirement, or per-user Blender step. Do not add the worker or builder in M7.
2. Normalize node transforms, scale and coordinate frame to M2's +X right, +Y up, +Z forward. Establish forward orientation and face confidence from the approved canonical/front view and projected/raycast landmarks. Use the M5 angles to disambiguate orientation; reject if correspondences are unreliable rather than inventing a front axis from topology.
3. Identify the facial surface with image landmarks plus geometry/texture checks. Do **not** interpret Hunyuan vertex indices as landmarks. Preserve hair, rear skull, ears, facial hair and accessories as identity geometry. Reject cases where these cannot be separated from the deformable region without losing the silhouette.
4. Use ICT FaceKit Light's **full facial region** as the initial rig source. Rigidly align landmarks, then nonrigidly fit the neutral face with landmark and surface constraints while protecting stylized eye size/spacing, nose, jaw and mouth placement. Transfer/adapt semantic expression deltas **on that fixed patch**. Do not force the whole face to stay inside a realistic PCA identity space. Position independent eyeballs from fitted sockets, retain/create an actual mouth opening and fitted interior, teeth and tongue.
5. Remove or hide only the corresponding Hunyuan facial surface. Join with a boundary/transition band that carries residual outer-boundary expression motion into a small shell neighborhood, matched normals and a texture projection/bake from the original UV appearance (use canonical view to resolve occluded/low-quality texels). Evaluate neutral and extreme poses from front, profile and rear; reject visible seams, penetrating hair/accessories, floating eyes, closed-mouth teeth, broken open jaw, implausible blink or identity drift. No camera-facing mask overlay is acceptable.
6. Optimize the rigid identity shell separately from the small morphable patch. Export a WebGL2-compatible GLB and explicit semantic manifest mapping `AvatarControlState` to head root, eyes, jaw and morph names. Keep `AvatarRigAdapter` the runtime boundary; MediaPipe remains in tracking interpretation. Measure load time, GPU memory and combined expressions in Safari and Chrome with multiple distinct M6 assets before M8 acceptance.

M8 must specifically prove automatic registration and seam/texture quality on **multiple independently generated GLBs** with different identities, hairstyles and facial proportions, including this confirmed sample. Compare their topology/UV reports; do not reuse a fitted boundary or vertex mapping between them. A clean neutral screenshot or a synthetic rig alone will not pass. If the hybrid fails these gates, revisit the ADR openly; the final 3D, 360° and facial-quality requirements do not relax.

## Limits of this research gate

- One original asset was measured structurally. No completed auto-fit or prepared avatar exists in M7. It is not yet demonstrated that the approach works well across people or even on this one asset.
- The reported `99%` OmniFaceRig success rate is for that project's screened benchmark, **not** a prediction for this application or Hunyuan output. Its published workflow is supporting evidence, not a dependency.
- Exact-position welding may merge coincident but semantically distinct surfaces; it is diagnostic only. No automatic face segmentation, eye center estimate, morph transfer or texture seam was quality-tested on this asset.
- Safari morph count, GPU memory, worker/CPU fitting time and WebGL2 rendering of the derived asset remain M8/M11 checks. glTF allows morph targets, but the [glTF 2.0 minimum client support](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html) does not guarantee all 53 shapes on every device; export only needed shapes or use validated packing as required.
